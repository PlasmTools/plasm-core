# Do not adopt catalogue round-robin

Both frozen arms completed all 40 variants and their reverse-insertion checks. Production was restored. No provider calls or AppWorld evaluation occurred.

The predeclared adoption screen fails: two baseline four-search passes are lost, 14 variants incur more than one additional search, and candidate boundary sensitivity reaches 15 searches. Aggregate availability nevertheless improves from 25/40 to 29/40 and requests fall from 234 to 210. Those totals conceal important regressions and are not a reason to adopt the rule.

Holding the 120 topical distractor operations fixed, candidate repeated membership and independent-information completion take one search with one distractor catalogue, three with four catalogues, and 16 with 16 or 120 catalogues. Merging distractors into the useful catalogue takes 13 searches in both arms. The candidate therefore neither solves within-catalogue starvation nor remains robust to catalogue partitioning.

The stronger counterexample is unrelated payroll records. Baseline completes every variant in one search. Candidate completion stretches to three searches for membership and four for independent information when payroll records occupy 16 or 120 catalogues. No semantic ambiguity in the fixture judgment is needed to produce this regression.

Exact first-search trace for remote/shards_016/parallel_information, repeated request: publication has lexical rank 3 in both arms, but candidate frontier rank 35; sample read has lexical rank 5 in both, but candidate frontier rank 69. Both become input-byte deferred before judgment. Baseline retains both in its first search. The opaque routine remains rank 1 and is retained as uncertain in both. This isolates the defect to scheduling/allocation rather than model rejection or lost constraints.

Design implication: catalogue identity is too arbitrary to determine an admission share. The earlier result still establishes a real concentration problem, but equal catalogue opportunity can override strong relevance and delay multiple independent useful operations. Keep the production scheduler unchanged. Retain these controls as a regression screen alongside the original starvation matrix.

Next design work should compare relevance-preserving, cost-aware progress over operation/acquisition candidates, with catalogue partition invariance as a design constraint. Do not invent another quota or tune a threshold on these results. First inspect where the existing relevance/frontier ordering loses progress and define a general invariant, then freeze a single candidate against the combined matrices. Caller use of already-exposed operations and terminal completion remain separate systems to validate.

This is a controlled development stress test with fixture judgment and inherited abstract contracts, not a sealed holdout, model-quality result or full-agent readiness claim.
